Ada Lovelace


Overview

Ada Lovelace (Dec 10, 1815 - Nov 27, 1852) was an English mathematician and a writer who was known as the "world's first computer programmer". She worked closely with Charles Babbage on their "Analytical Engine", which was an early concept of a computer.

What did she contribute?

Lovelace helped establish the idea of computer programming. She created an algorithm for the "Analytical Engine". Beyond just being able to calculate numbers she had imagined the limitless potential that computers could have.

Why was she important?

Lovelace was important because she had imagined the potential of computers before they existed. Her combination of mathematics and imagination allowed herself to see possibilites that not even her partner Babbage could've imagined. All while women at this time were still strongly discouraged from participating in science and mathematics.

  • 1833 - Met Charles Babbage
  • 1842 - Published translation of "Notes on the Analytical Engine"
  • 1843 - Wrote the first computer program
